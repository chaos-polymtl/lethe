## [Master] - 2026/11/04

### Changed

- MINOR `Shape::distance_to_shape` and `Shape::distance_to_shape_with_cell_guess` are renamed `half_distance_to_shape` and `half_distance_to_shape_with_cell_guess`. They return the level set of both shapes at the point where these level sets are equal, which is half the distance between the two shapes, and the old name led the particle-particle contact of the sharp immersed-boundary solver to use it as the full distance. The functions themselves are unchanged. [#PR](link)

### Fixed

- MAJOR The particle-particle contact of the sharp immersed-boundary solver used half the overlap of the two particles in the Hertz model, which expects the full overlap, as the particle-wall contact already did. The normal force was therefore 2^(-3/2), about 0.35, of the Hertz force for a given overlap. This changes the results of all the cases with particle-particle contacts. [#PR](link)
- MAJOR The lubrication force of the sharp immersed-boundary solver projected the relative velocity on the vector joining the two particles, or the particle and the wall, instead of the unit vector. The force was therefore multiplied by the distance between the centers, or between the center and the wall, and depended on the length unit. In addition, the particle-wall force used the square of the radius instead of the square of the diameter, as the documented limit of an infinite second diameter gives. The force on a sphere approaching a wall is now 6 pi mu R^2 U / h. This changes the results of any simulation in which coupled particles come within the `lubrication range max` of each other or of a wall. [#PR](link)

### Documentation

- MINOR The documentation of the lubrication force of the sharp immersed-boundary solver now gives the 1/h_max term, which makes the force vanish at the end of the lubrication range, the orientation of the relative velocity and of the unit vector, and the particle-wall limit of the force. [#PR](link)
