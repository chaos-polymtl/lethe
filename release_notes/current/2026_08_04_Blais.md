## [Master] - 2026/08/04

### Fixed

- MAJOR The velocity Verlet scheme never carried out its opening half step, so `lethe-particles`, `lethe-fluid-particles` and `lethe-fluid-particles-matrix-free` were effectively running a first-order symplectic Euler scheme instead of the second-order scheme they reported. The three solvers guarded the half step with `simulation_control->get_iteration_number() == 0`, but `SimulationControlTransient::integrate()` increments the iteration number before the body of the time loop runs, so the counter is always greater than or equal to one inside the loop and the branch was dead code. The scheme is now completed on both ends. The opening step advances the velocities by half a time step and the positions by a full time step, which starts the staggering without losing any physical time, and the closing step re-evaluates the forces at the final position of the particles and advances the velocities by half a time step, which synchronizes them with the positions. Completing `n` time steps therefore requires `n+1` integration operations. In pure DEM, the closing step is carried out once the time loop is over and is followed by an additional particle statistics report, announced as `Synchronized particle statistics` and subject to the usual `log frequency`, which reports the velocities at the same time as the positions. In CFD-DEM, it is carried out at the end of the DEM sub-iterations of every CFD time step, so that the drag and the post-processing of the next CFD time step see velocities that are synchronized with the positions rather than lagging by half a DEM time step. Two latent bugs in the opening step were fixed at the same time, both of which only became reachable once the step started executing: it did not reset the force and the torque containers, which the contact force objects accumulate into and which would therefore have leaked into the second time step, and it advanced only `dim` velocity and angular velocity components rather than three, which in 2D skipped `omega_z`, the only axis of rotation there is. The restart path is unaffected, since checkpoints are written inside the time loop and therefore store the staggered velocities that a restarted run resumes from. Note that particles inserted after the first time step still receive a full velocity step rather than an opening half step, which remains to be addressed. A `velocity_verlet_free_fall` application test, which monitors the velocity magnitude of a single particle in free fall through the DEM statistics, and a rewritten `integration_velocity_verlet` unit test, which checks the complete opening, regular and closing sequence in 2D and 3D against the analytical solution of a uniformly accelerated motion, were added. The parameter documentation of `integration method` and the velocity Verlet section of the DEM theory guide were updated accordingly. This changes the results of every DEM and CFD-DEM simulation that uses the velocity Verlet scheme, which is the default, and the golden output of the affected application tests was regenerated. [#](https://github.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/pull/)

### Changed

- MAJOR The `Integrator` interface now exposes the three stages of a time integration scheme as `integrate_start`, `integrate` and `integrate_end`, instead of the previous `integrate_half_step_location` and `integrate` pair. The solvers call `integrate_start` at the first time step, `integrate` at every subsequent one and `integrate_end` once the final position is reached, without ever querying which scheme is in use: it is up to the scheme, and no longer to the caller, to decide what an opening and a closing step mean. `VelocityVerletIntegrator` implements them as the opening and the closing half velocity steps described above, whereas `ExplicitEulerIntegrator`, which is self-starting and keeps its velocities synchronized with its positions, implements `integrate_start` as a regular step and `integrate_end` as a reset of the force and the torque containers. That reset is part of the contract of every integration entry point and is not optional: the force evaluation which precedes `integrate_end` fills the containers, and the contact force objects accumulate into them, so leaving them untouched would double-count the forces of the next time step. An adaptive-sparse-contacts overload of `integrate_end` was added for symmetry with `integrate`; it advances only the particles of the mobile cells and resets the force and the torque of the others. The `torque` and `force` arguments of `integrate_start` are no longer `const`, since the opening step now resets them as well. [#](https://github.com/chaos-polymtl/lethe/pull/)
