## [Master] - 2026/10/22

### Added

- MINOR Eleven examples were added to the automatic validation of Lethe on Lucille (`contrib/validation/validation_cases_lucille.txt`). They each run in less than an hour and compare against an analytical solution, a correlation, other simulations or experimental data: the melting cavity with the stasis constraint (Gau and Viskanta), the capillary wave (Prosperetti), the 2D dam break (Martin and Moyce), the 2D sandpile formation with the EPSD, constant and viscous rolling resistance models (Ai et al. and Zuriguel et al.), the oblique wall impact (Kharaz et al. and MFIX), the sudden-expansion flow at Re=70 and Re=610 (Durst et al.), the warming up of a viscous fluid (analytical), the cooling fin (analytical), the waveguide (analytical TE10 mode), the 3D turbulent Taylor-Couette flow (Wang and Jourdan) and the sedimentation of one particle (ten Cate et al.). Every example has a `validate.sh` script, and its post-processing script has a `--validate` option that stores the plotted data in `.dat` files and saves the figures as PDF without displaying them. Three post-processing scripts are new: `waveguide-postprocessing.py`, which reads the table of errors printed by Lethe and plots their convergence; `post-process-sedimentation-1-particle.py`, which compares the velocity of the particle with the E4 experiment of ten Cate et al.; and `compare_solution.py` of the cooling fin, which now also stores the heat fluxes. The velocity of the E4 experiment, `ten-cate-E4.dat`, was extracted from Fig. 8 of Barbeau et al. (2024). Some parameter files were adapted: `melting-cavity-stasis-constraint.prm` now computes the geometric melt volume, which `melting-cavity.py` requires; the three parameter files of the sandpile formation write their checkpoints under different names, so that they can run at the same time; and the 3D turbulent Taylor-Couette flow now writes its results less often. In validation mode, the post-processing of the sudden-expansion flow at Re=610 mirrors the velocity profiles when the jet attaches to the other wall, since that wall is chosen by round-off errors.
