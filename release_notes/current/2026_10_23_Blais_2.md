## [Master] - 2026/10/22

### Added

- MINOR Eleven examples were added to the automatic validation of Lethe on Lucille (`contrib/validation/validation_cases_lucille.txt`). They each run in less than an hour and compare against an analytical solution, a correlation, other simulations or experimental data: the melting cavity with the stasis constraint (Gau and Viskanta), the capillary wave at 15 times the capillary time-step constraint (Prosperetti), the 2D dam break (Martin and Moyce), the 2D sandpile formation with the EPSD, constant and viscous rolling resistance models (Ai et al. and Zuriguel et al.), the oblique wall impact (Kharaz et al. and MFIX), the sudden-expansion flow at Re=70 and Re=610 (Durst et al.), the warming up of a viscous fluid (analytical), the cooling fin (analytical), the waveguide (analytical TE10 mode), the 3D turbulent Taylor-Couette flow (Wang and Jourdan) and the sedimentation of one particle (ten Cate et al.). Every example has a `validate.sh` script, and its post-processing script has a `--validate` option that stores the plotted data in `.dat` files and saves the figures as PDF without displaying them. Three post-processing scripts are new: `waveguide-postprocessing.py`, which reads the table of errors printed by Lethe and plots their convergence; `post-process-sedimentation-1-particle.py`, which compares the velocity of the particle with the E4 experiment of ten Cate et al.; and `compare_solution.py` of the cooling fin, which now also stores the heat fluxes. The velocity of the E4 experiment, `ten-cate-E4.dat`, was digitized from the original figure of the documentation of this example. A parameter file, `capillary-wave-TSM-15.prm`, was added for the capillary wave at 15 times the capillary time-step constraint. Some parameter files were adapted: `melting-cavity-stasis-constraint.prm` now computes the geometric melt volume, which `melting-cavity.py` requires; the three parameter files of the sandpile formation write their checkpoints under different names, so that they can run at the same time; the 3D turbulent Taylor-Couette flow now writes its results less often; and the sedimentation of one particle now uses a non-linear tolerance of 1e-5, a GMRES relative residual of 1e-5 with at most 200 iterations and Krylov vectors, and an ILU absolute tolerance of 1e-12. In validation mode, the post-processing of the sudden-expansion flow at Re=610 mirrors the velocity profiles when the jet attaches to the other wall, since that wall is chosen by round-off errors.
